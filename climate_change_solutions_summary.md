**Key Climate‑Change Solutions – Easy‑to‑remember Points**

| # | What to Do | Why It Helps |
|---|------------|--------------|
| **1** | **Stop digging and burning fossil fuels** | Coal, oil and gas release the most carbon. The sooner they’re left underground, the faster we cut emissions. |
| **2** | **Go clean‑energy** | Solar, wind, tidal, wave and geothermal power produce almost no greenhouse gases. Switching to these fuels keeps the planet from heating further. |
| **3** | **Use greener transport** | Switch cars to electric, walk or cycle more, and reduce air/sea travel. Fossil‑fuel vehicles are a huge source of pollution. |
| **4** | **Make homes energy‑efficient** | Insulate walls & roofs, replace old boilers with heat pumps, and keep heating at sensible levels. Saves money and cuts emissions. |
| **5** | **Eat less meat & dairy** | Animal farming is a top contributor to CO₂, methane and other gases. Plant‑based diets use far fewer resources and produce less waste. |
| **6** | **Restore and protect nature** | Plant trees, rewild grasslands, and preserve wetlands. These ecosystems soak up CO₂ and lock it in soil. |
| **7** | **Defend forests** | Stop illegal logging and large‑scale clear‑cutting, especially in places like the Amazon. Whole forests act as massive carbon sinks. |
| **8** | **Guard the oceans** | Keep marine areas free from overfishing, drilling and deep‑sea mining. Oceans absorb a huge amount of CO₂ and keep the climate balanced. |
| **9** | **Buy less, choose well** | Reduce overall consumption (clothes, gadgets, food). Over‑production creates extra emissions and waste. |
| **10** | **Cut plastic use** | Plastics come from oil and are energy‑intensive to make and dispose of. Less plastic means less carbon in the supply chain. |

**Extra context**

* The **Paris Agreement (2015)** is the global treaty that pushes all countries toward the same goals.  
* **Net‑zero emissions** means the amount of CO₂ we emit equals the amount removed or offset—our ultimate target for climate stability.  

By combining all these actions—policy, business, and personal choices—we can slow, stop, and eventually reverse the worst effects of climate change.
